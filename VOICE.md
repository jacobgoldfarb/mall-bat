# Mall Bats Voice

Fall 2004. A dying mall, a crew of squatters, a ceiling full of plywood. The game is funny because the world plays it straight: the PA is chipper, Gary is lonely, the Tamagotchi needs feeding. The Bats never wink at the player.

## The register

- **Deadpan, specific, warm.** "Your cart is in the fountain." Real nouns beat adjectives: Cinnabomb, Yellowcard, a Dave Matthews sticker.
- **The world talks, the UI doesn't.** Jokes live in flavor lines, PA announcements, logs and events. Mechanics text is plain and short.
- **One joke per surface.** If a line has a joke, it doesn't also need an exclamation mark, a pun and a pop-culture reference.
- **Never explain the joke, never sell it.** No "kooky", "hilarious", "chaos ensues".
- **2004 is set dressing, not a quiz.** One period detail per screen at most.

## Mechanics copy

Tell players what to *do* and what it's *for*. Let them discover how it works.

- **Item desc:** one line, about 12 words. Lead with the effect players will feel. Give a number only when it's the decision (comfort, watts, beds).
- **Tooltip:** at most 2 short lines. State the lever and the risk, not the formula.
- **Help:** 2–3 sentences per topic. Teach the loop, not the edge cases.
- **Logs:** past tense, a name and a consequence. "Gary pocketed the Tamagotchi."

Cut on sight:

- Restating what the tab, icon or label already says ("A Bat sets it up on the mall floor" in the Mall tab).
- Parenthetical rules ("(raises Suspicion a little)", "(5 parts to clear)").
- "Separate from X", "which means", "so that", "Note:", "Game over."
- Chains of clauses joined by colons and semicolons.
- Exact formulas and percentages players can't act on (60% at 0, 120% at 100).
- Every exception listed up front ("unless scrutiny is high. Fire marshals and sweeps ignore it.").
- Narrating the design. If a line reads like a spec or a changelog ("Overworked Bats sour the crew's mood", "Everything earned counts, even after you spend it", "Route: … Risk: …"), it was written for the developer. Say what the player would say about it, or say nothing.
- Explaining hidden systems. Some rules are meant to be felt, like what drives levels or how Heat ramps on a repeated job. Show the meter and let the player work it out.

## Discovery vs. confusion

- **Say it:** what a thing is for, what it costs, what can go wrong. Blocking reasons ("Every Workbench is taken").
- **Show it:** numbers that change (yield, Heat, chance) go in meters and previews, not prose.
- **Let them find out:** secondary rules (the janitor folds signs up, guards pocket decoys, excuses wear thin). Reveal them through a log line the first time they happen, so it reads as a story beat.
- **Never hide a fail state.** If something can get a Bat arrested or end the run, the player hears about it before it happens.

## Names

- The crew are **the Bats**, the ceiling is **home**, the mall floor is **the mall**.
- **Suspicion** is the mall's. **Heat** is a Bat's. Never "alert", "recognition" or "familiarity".
- Staff by name: Gary, Officer Dana, the janitor, Dale from Maintenance.
